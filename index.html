Kuiyi (Cory) Gao

[Focus] I work on a practical question: how can we tell when an AI system is reliable, and what should happen when it is not? My recent work covers GUI agents, runtime checks for third-party agent skills, and multimodal safety.

[How I work] I like turning a broad question into something testable. That usually means building retrieval or agent pipelines, evaluation code, automated judges, traces, and figures needed to inspect a system's behavior.

[Background] I am a computer science undergraduate at UNC-Chapel Hill, graduating in May 2027. I have worked with Tencent AI Lab, CUHK, and MBZUAI UGRIP.

Research

RealGUINoise: An Interactive Cross-Platform Benchmark for GUI Agent Robustness under Real-World Interface Noise

Co-first author. Under review.

What changes when a GUI agent meets a popup, delayed content, or a look-alike control? We run the same web and Android tasks on clean and altered interfaces. I built tasks, the automated judge, and the paired evaluation.

arXiv

RuntimeTickets: Source-Grounded Security Obligations for Agent Skills

First author. Under review at ICLR 2027.

RuntimeTickets checks agent tool use before execution and records PASS, BLOCK, or DEFER decisions with the observed effect. I designed the method, built the runtime layer and reviewable traces with the UGRIP team, and lead the follow-up experiments and paper.

Can't See the Forest for the Trees: Benchmarking Multimodal Safety Awareness for Multimodal LLMs

Wenxuan Wang, Xiaoyuan Liu, Kuiyi Gao, Jen-tse Huang, Youliang Yuan, Pinjia He, Shuai Wang, Zhaopeng Tu

ACL 2025

This benchmark asks whether multimodal models notice risks in images without treating harmless inputs as unsafe. As third author, I worked on data, evaluation, fine-tuning, error analysis, submission, and rebuttal.

Chain-of-Jailbreak Attack for Image Generation Models via Step by Step Editing

Wenxuan Wang, Kuiyi Gao, Youliang Yuan, Jen-tse Huang, Qiuzhi Liu, Shuai Wang, Wenxiang Jiao, Zhaopeng Tu

Findings of ACL 2025

This study looks at safety failures that appear over several image-editing turns. As second author, I designed the attack and prompting defense, contributed data, ran most experiments, and prepared figures, writing, and rebuttal.

Natural language processing course project, UNC

MemSlot treats long-document question answering as a retrieval and compression problem before OCR. I built the neural retriever, document rendering, pruning baselines, and evaluation notebooks.
